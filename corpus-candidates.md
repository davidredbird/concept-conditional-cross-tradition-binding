# Corpus Expansion Candidates (v1)

Sources to investigate, evaluate, and potentially include in the rigorous v1 corpus. The v0 corpus (see `corpus/README.md`) was deliberately small and Anglo-Western-centric for fast prototyping. v1 should be broader, deeper, and better-controlled.

This is a planning document, not a final inclusion list. For each entry: **(what it is, why it's interesting, what to verify before including).**

---

## Operating principle: mine widely

Any discipline that may be describing the same structural features in its own vocabulary is a candidate for inclusion. The strategic argument:

- The skeptical-scientist objection to mining contemplative traditions is "that stuff isn't science."
- That objection collapses if we can demonstrate, rigorously, that a structural claim made by tradition X (in religious vocabulary) is the same claim being made by science Y (in scientific vocabulary).
- The convergence becomes its own credibility-laundering: religious claims get scientific cover, scientific claims gain historical depth, and the broader inquiry becomes legitimate.
- **The corpus should therefore include not only contemplative traditions but *any field* generating texts that arguably express the same structural features.** Let the analysis decide whether the agreement is real.

The categories below reflect this: classical contemplative traditions, plus modern scientific/computational framings, plus borderline disciplines worth probing.

## Nondual category — known gaps in v0

### Christian mystical (expand beyond Eckhart / John of the Cross / Pseudo-Dionysius)

- **The Cloud of Unknowing** (14c anonymous English) — apophatic contemplative classic, structurally close to Eckhart.
- **Jacob Boehme** (17c) — Christian theosophy, nondual in places, weird and rich.
- **Teresa of Ávila** — *Interior Castle*. More devotional than nondual but contains union-with-God material.
- **Brother Lawrence** — *The Practice of the Presence of God*. Less theoretical, more experiential.
- **Thomas Merton** — *New Seeds of Contemplation*. 20c Catholic monk explicitly engaging Buddhist nondualism.
- **Bernadette Roberts** — *The Experience of No-Self*. 20c Catholic contemplative whose reports map closely to Buddhist/Advaita descriptions.

### Eastern Orthodox / Patristic

- **Gregory of Nyssa** — *Life of Moses*, *On the Soul and Resurrection*. Apophatic theology, infinite divine darkness.
- **Maximus the Confessor** — *Mystagogy*. Cosmos as theophany, deification (theosis).
- **The Philokalia** — multi-author compilation of Eastern Christian contemplative texts.
- **Hesychast tradition** — Gregory Palamas on uncreated light, divine energies vs. essence. Subtle nondual structure.

### Ethiopian Orthodox / Coptic / African Christianity

The **Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo** canon is the broadest in Christianity (~81 books vs. 66 Protestant / 73 Catholic). Mel Gibson's recent attention has brought it into popular discussion. Worth investigating:

- **1 Enoch** (in Ethiopian canon, not in Western Bibles) — Second Temple Jewish apocalyptic, cosmological visions, the Watchers, heavenly ascents. Not straightforwardly nondual, but rich material on cosmology and theophany worth examining for structural overlap.
- **Jubilees** — alternate Genesis chronology; mostly historical, probably not nondual.
- **Meqabyan I-III** (Ethiopian Maccabees) — historical/devotional; likely not relevant to convergence test.
- **Book of the Ethiopian Mystic-Saints** — desert tradition descended from Egyptian/Coptic Christianity. Worth tracing.
- **Abba Giyorgis of Sagla** (14c Ethiopian theologian-mystic) — wrote on the divine essence in ways that may parallel Pseudo-Dionysius and the apophatic tradition.
- **The Ethiopian Synaxarium** — hagiographic readings; mostly devotional but contains contemplative themes worth scanning.

**Verification needed:** Most Ethiopian sources require translation work that isn't widely available in English. The strongest candidate from this tradition for the corpus is probably anything in the *apophatic/contemplative* lineage (Abba Giyorgis, Ethiopian desert father traditions descended from Antony and the Egyptian desert), not the canonical-Bible expansions per se. The canonical expansions (1 Enoch etc.) are interesting in their own right but their primary content is apocalyptic/cosmological rather than contemplative. This tradition deserves a dedicated research pass.

### Sufi (expand beyond Ibn Arabi / Rumi / al-Hallaj)

- **Junayd of Baghdad** (9c) — sober school of Sufism; fana (annihilation in God) doctrine.
- **al-Ghazali** — *Ihya' Ulum al-Din*, *The Niche of Lights*. Often dualistic, but the contemplative sections matter.
- **Suhrawardi** — Illuminationist philosophy, light metaphysics.
- **Bistami** (9c) — ecstatic utterances (shathiyat) parallel to al-Hallaj.
- **Attar** — *Conference of the Birds*. Allegorical, but the Simurgh ending is explicitly nondual.

### Buddhist (expand beyond Dzogchen)

- **Heart Sutra** with commentary (Red Pine ed.) — short, explicitly nondual.
- **Diamond Sutra** — central Mahayana text on emptiness.
- **Lankavatara Sutra** — mind-only doctrine, influenced Chan/Zen.
- **Hua-yen / Avatamsaka** — Indra's Net imagery, mutual interpenetration.
- **Hongzhi Zhengjue** — silent illumination Chan.
- **Dogen** — *Shobogenzo*. Zen, structurally nondual.
- **Bankei** — Unborn Mind teachings.
- **Huangbo** — *Transmission of Mind*. Classic Chan nondualism.

### Hindu (expand beyond Shankara / Ramana / Nisargadatta)

- **Upanishads** — primary source for Advaita; Brihadaranyaka, Chandogya, Mandukya especially. Use Shankara's commentary editions.
- **Ashtavakra Gita** — sharper and more uncompromising than Bhagavad Gita.
- **Yoga Vasistha** — long, contains powerful nondual passages.
- **Ribhu Gita** — recommended by Ramana.
- **Tripura Rahasya** — Tantric nondual.

### Kabbalah (expand beyond Zohar / Tanya)

- **Sefer Yetzirah** — early Jewish mystical cosmology, hard to place but worth scanning.
- **Moses de León** — primary Zohar attribution; deeper engagement with the Zohar.
- **Abraham Abulafia** — prophetic Kabbalah, more experiential than theoretical Kabbalah.
- **Isaac Luria** — *Etz Chaim*. Lurianic system (tzimtzum, shevirat ha-kelim, tikkun) is structurally rich.
- **Hasidic masters** beyond Schneur Zalman: Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev, Nachman of Breslov, the Maggid of Mezeritch.

### Daoist / Chinese (expand)

- **Tao Te Ching** in multiple translations (Mitchell, Lau, Ames-Hall) — translator variance test natural here.
- **Liezi** — alongside Zhuangzi for early Daoism.
- **Inner Chapters of Zhuangzi** fully, not just excerpts.
- **Wang Bi commentary** on Tao Te Ching — Neo-Daoist nondual reading.
- **Chinul** — Korean Seon master; cross-cultural Zen.

## Modern scientific/computational nondual analogs (Category 1b)

Contemporary thinkers who arrived at structurally nondual conclusions via non-contemplative methods. **Already partially included in v0 corpus.** Worth expansion for v1.

### Simulation theory and computational ontology

- **Nick Bostrom**, "Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?" (*Philosophical Quarterly*, 2003) — canonical. Substrate independence + ancestor simulations.
- **David Chalmers**, "The Matrix as Metaphysics" (2003) and *Reality+* (2022) — careful philosophical treatment of simulation hypothesis as metaphysics.
- **Robin Hanson**, "How to Live in a Simulation" — practical implications, sharper framing.
- **Nick Bostrom**, subsequent follow-up papers responding to objections.

### Information physics

- **John Archibald Wheeler**, "Information, Physics, Quantum: The Search for Links" (1989) — "it from bit."
- **Wheeler**, "Law without Law" — participatory universe.
- **Seth Lloyd**, *Programming the Universe* (2006).
- **Gerard 't Hooft**, papers on dimensional reduction / holographic principle.
- **Leonard Susskind**, *The Black Hole War*; technical papers on holographic principle.
- **Jacob Bekenstein**, original bounds on information in physical regions.
- **David Deutsch**, *The Fabric of Reality*; constructor theory papers.

### Mathematical universe / Platonist physics

- **Max Tegmark**, "The Mathematical Universe" (*Foundations of Physics*, 2007); *Our Mathematical Universe* (2014).
- **Tegmark**'s Level IV multiverse papers.
- **Roger Penrose**, *The Road to Reality* — relevant chapters on mathematical Platonism in physics.

### Analytic idealism / contemporary panpsychism

- **Bernardo Kastrup**, *Why Materialism Is Baloney* (2014), *The Idea of the World* (2019), *Decoding Schopenhauer's Metaphysics* (2020).
- **Philip Goff**, *Galileo's Error* (2019); *Why? The Purpose of the Universe* (2023).
- **Galen Strawson**, "Realistic Monism" (2006) — contemporary panpsychism.
- **Hedda Hassel Mørch** — papers on IIT and panpsychism.

### Interface theory and perception-as-construction

- **Donald Hoffman**, *The Case Against Reality* (2019); "The Interface Theory of Perception" papers.
- **Hoffman + Prakash**, mathematical conscious-agent papers.

### Predictive processing / free energy principle (adjacent — see notes)

These are *not* obviously nondual, but they make claims about observer-environment continuity, the constructed nature of perception, and the brain's modeling of the world that overlap with nondual descriptions. Worth a category of their own.

- **Karl Friston**, free energy principle papers; *Active Inference* (2022, with Parr and Pezzulo).
- **Andy Clark**, *Surfing Uncertainty* (2015); *The Experience Machine* (2023).
- **Anil Seth**, *Being You* (2021) — controlled hallucination framing.
- **Jakob Hohwy**, *The Predictive Mind* (2013).

**Predicted experimental result:** these texts may form a *bridge cluster* — closer to nondual than to dualistic, but not identical to nondual. They describe observer-environment continuity without making the full ontological claim. If true, that's its own interesting finding.

### Integrated information theory / consciousness as substrate-feature

- **Giulio Tononi**, *Phi: A Voyage from the Brain to the Soul* (2012); IIT papers.
- **Christof Koch**, *The Feeling of Life Itself* (2019).
- **Tononi + Koch**, "Consciousness: Here, There and Everywhere?" (2015).

### Complexity, emergence, and self-organization

- **Stuart Kauffman**, *Reinventing the Sacred* (2008); *A World Beyond Physics* (2019).
- **Sara Walker + Lee Cronin**, assembly theory papers (2017–present).
- **Stephen Wolfram**, *A New Kind of Science* (2002); Physics Project material.
- **Ilya Prigogine**, *Order Out of Chaos*.

### Relational and observer-dependent physics

- **Carlo Rovelli**, *Helgoland* (2020) — relational quantum mechanics; *The Order of Time*.
- **Lee Smolin**, *Three Roads to Quantum Gravity*; *The Trouble with Physics*.
- **Wheeler/Zurek**, *Quantum Theory and Measurement* (anthology).

### Process philosophy and proto-systems thought

- **Alfred North Whitehead**, *Process and Reality*; *Science and the Modern World*.
- **David Bohm**, *Wholeness and the Implicate Order* (1980) — already nondual-adjacent, would have been a contender for Category 1a.
- **Henri Bergson**, *Creative Evolution* — duration, élan vital.

## Dualistic-contemplative controls — expand

- **Eastern Orthodox texts that are explicitly dualistic** (substance-distinct from creation). Most Orthodox theology emphasizes the Creator/creature distinction sharply.
- **Catholic neo-Thomism** post-Aquinas — Suárez, John of St. Thomas, Gilson, Maritain.
- **Madhyamika** (Nagarjuna) — emptiness, but in a way that explicitly resists nondual identification. *This is a hard, important control* — Madhyamika is sometimes read as nondual and sometimes as the rigorous refusal of any positive metaphysics including nondualism. Worth two passes: one as control, one as ambiguous case.
- **Yogacara / Vasubandhu** — mind-only, but more dualistic than Mahayana absolutist readings.
- **Pure Land Buddhism** — devotional, dualistic Amida/devotee structure.
- **Theravada beyond Abhidhamma** — Mahasi Sayadaw, Pa Auk Sayadaw, modern Theravada teaching.
- **Reformed / Calvinist theology** — strict Creator-creature distinction, sovereign-vs-creature.
- **Process theology** (Whitehead, Hartshorne, Cobb) — God-world relationship, not classical dualism but distinctively non-monist.

## Non-contemplative controls — expand

- **Logical positivism** — Carnap, Ayer.
- **Quine** — *Two Dogmas of Empiricism*, *Word and Object*.
- **Wittgenstein** — *Tractatus* and *Philosophical Investigations* are interesting opposite poles.
- **Modern philosophy of mind** that explicitly rejects mystical/contemplative framings — Dennett (*Consciousness Explained*), Churchland (*Neurophilosophy*).
- **Pragmatism** — James (carefully — *Varieties of Religious Experience* would belong elsewhere; we want the *Pragmatism* lectures).
- **Hegel selective passages** — *Phenomenology of Spirit*. Risk: Hegel writes about Absolute Spirit in ways that smell nondual. *Borderline case* — including him as a control would actually be informative regardless of outcome.

## Adversarial / "false positive" controls

For the v1 rigorous run, we want texts that *sound* nondual but aren't, to catch the embedding model latching onto surface style:

- **New Age / contemporary spiritual literature** that uses nondual vocabulary loosely — Tolle, Deepak Chopra, modern non-duality teachers without classical rigor. The risk: these *are* genuinely nondual in content, just less rigorous. Need to think carefully about whether to include or exclude.
- **LLM-generated synthetic mystical text** prompted to imitate each tradition. If real cross-tradition clustering isn't tighter than synthetic clustering, the apparent agreement is a vocabulary effect.
- **Pop-spirituality summaries** — Wikipedia-style descriptions of nondual traditions. These exist outside the contemplative current and should cluster differently if the embeddings are picking up something deeper than vocabulary.

## Hard cases worth their own analysis

These don't fit cleanly into nondual/dualistic and may deserve their own category or sensitivity analyses:

- **Madhyamika / Nagarjuna** — see above. Anti-positionalism that resists both poles.
- **Quietists / pietists** — close to mystical but doctrinally vary.
- **Theosophy / Anthroposophy** (Blavatsky, Steiner) — explicit perennialist synthesis. *Should not be in the test corpus* because they were constructed *with* the convergence hypothesis in mind; using them would smuggle in the conclusion. Useful as a meta-control: do they cluster with the genuine nondual texts they synthesized?
- **Christian Science** (Eddy) — claims nondual structure but in a very particular vocabulary.
- **Modern Western nondual teachers** — Rupert Spira, Adyashanti, etc. Genuine but contemporary; risk of cross-cultural contamination by globalized spiritual culture. Include with care, marked as 20-21c.

## Translator diversity (the biggest v1 priority)

For every nondual source we include, find at least 2-3 different published translations. This is *the* most important methodological upgrade from v0. Examples worth pursuing:

- **Tao Te Ching**: Mitchell, Lau, Ames-Hall, Watson, Henricks, Red Pine.
- **Heart Sutra**: Conze, Red Pine, Tanahashi, Suzuki.
- **Plotinus Enneads**: MacKenna, Armstrong (Loeb), Gerson.
- **Eckhart sermons**: Walshe, McGinn, Davies.
- **Bhagavad Gita** (if included): Easwaran, Mitchell, Miller, Sargeant.
- **Upanishads**: Olivelle, Easwaran, Müller.

Translator-shuffled control analyses (does swapping translator change the cluster assignment?) become possible with this kind of redundancy.

## Inclusion criteria — to be codified before v1 pre-registration

Right now "nondual" is determined by investigator judgment. For v1, codify:

1. **Doctrinal criterion**: text or its tradition makes one of {observer-substrate non-separability, denial of independent self, immanence of ultimate in particulars, absence of any privileged distinction between knower and known} as a core claim.
2. **Scholarly attestation**: at least two independent academic sources characterize the text/author as nondual (or as engaging with nondual themes).
3. **Author intent**: the author understood themselves as making metaphysical/contemplative claims, not just devotional or literary ones.
4. **Exclude polemics**: texts arguing *for* nondualism against another position should be category-distinct from texts *expressing* nondualism. (Polemics will have a different rhetorical structure that embeddings may catch.)

The dualistic-control criterion should mirror the nondual one: explicit Creator-creature distinction, substance dualism, real distinction of essences, etc.

## Practical research notes for future corpus iteration

- The **Tibetan Buddhist Resource Center** has many texts available online with English summaries.
- **PhilPapers** and **Academia.edu** searches surface scholarly editions and free-access translations.
- **Sefaria** has extensive Jewish text material in Hebrew + English.
- **Internet Sacred Text Archive** has many public-domain translations but they're often old and need verification.
- **JSTOR** for scholarly commentary on inclusion decisions.
- For the Ethiopian Orthodox tradition specifically: **Aethiopica** (academic journal), works by **Ephraim Isaac**, and the **Ethiopian Manuscript Microfilm Library** at HMML. This is a less-mined corpus and could be a real differentiator if the project goes there.
