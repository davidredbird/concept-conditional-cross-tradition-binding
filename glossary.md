# Glossary / Translation-Table Seed

A translation table: each candidate convergent structural feature gets a neutral name, an operational definition, a detection method, and a mapping to the vocabulary of each tradition that expresses it.

This document is the **v0 seed** — the pre-registered candidate features (see `paper/paper-draft-v5.md` Appendix A), fleshed out with provisional definitions and tradition-mappings. Each entry is a starting hypothesis subject to refinement or falsification by analysis. Some entries survive analysis as validated features; others collapse into more general ones; some are replaced by features that emerge from the data.

**Status convention:**
- `seed` — pre-registered candidate, no empirical validation yet.
- `provisional` — appears in v0 prototype analysis; needs Phase 1 validation.
- `validated` — survives all controls in rigorous run.
- `revised` — re-formulated based on findings.
- `refuted` — does not survive controls; preserved for transparency.

All entries below are currently `seed`.

## Feature 1: Observer-substrate non-separability

**Status:** seed

**Neutral statement:** The boundary between the entity that perceives/knows and the substrate of what is perceived/known is not metaphysically fundamental. Apparent separation is a property of the representation, not the system.

**Operational definition:** A text expresses this feature when it (a) denies that consciousness is a distinct thing from its objects, (b) claims that observer and observed share a single substrate, or (c) describes the perceived separation as an artifact of cognition rather than a fact about reality.

**Detection method (planned):** Contrastive direction in embedding space — compute (nondual − dualistic) mean vectors; test whether passages claiming observer-substrate non-separability load high on this direction across traditions.

**Tradition expressions:**
- **Advaita:** *tat tvam asi* ("thou art that"); identity of atman and Brahman.
- **Dzogchen:** rigpa as the ground of both perception and the perceived; no perceiver outside what is perceived.
- **Christian mystical (Eckhart):** "The eye through which I see God is the same eye through which God sees me."
- **Sufi (Ibn Arabi):** wahdat al-wujud; the cosmos as the outward face of the Real and the human as its inward face.
- **Neoplatonism:** soul and the One coincident in the highest union; "flight of the alone to the Alone."
- **Kabbalah:** *ein od milvado* ("there is nothing besides Him").
- **Daoism:** "Heaven and earth came into being with me, and the ten thousand things and I are one."

**Modern parallels worth investigating:**
- Observer-dependence in quantum measurement (Wigner, relational QM).
- The hard problem of consciousness and its dissolution under nondual views.
- IIT's identification of consciousness with integrated information (system-substrate identity).

**Predicted measurable consequence:** Subjects with deep contemplative training should show altered subject-object processing in perceptual tasks (e.g., reduced figure-ground rigidity, altered DMN activity during self-referential thought). Partially supported by existing meditation neuroscience.

## Feature 2: Absence of a privileged self

**Status:** seed

**Neutral statement:** There is no observer-entity behind the observation; the apparent center of experience is itself a phenomenon arising in experience, not its source.

**Operational definition:** A text expresses this feature when it (a) denies the existence of a substantial self, (b) describes the "I" as constructed or as an arising phenomenon, or (c) claims that careful investigation fails to locate any independent observer.

**Note on distinction from Feature 1:** Non-self can be expressed *with* or *without* observer-substrate non-separability. Theravada Abhidhamma asserts non-self while explicitly *denying* observer-substrate identification — the aggregates are conditioned and pass away, but they are not "one substance with reality." So this feature appears in nondual *and* in some dualistic traditions. The experiment must catch this nuance.

**Tradition expressions:**
- **Advaita:** the empirical self is unreal; only Atman/Brahman is real.
- **Dzogchen:** no findable "looker" apart from looking; the seeker *is* what is sought.
- **Theravada (control case):** *anatta*; no enduring self among the five aggregates. **Note:** in Theravada this is paired with denial of substrate-identification, so the feature partially overlaps and partially diverges.
- **Christian mystical:** "I pray God to rid me of God" (Eckhart) — radical emptying of the self.
- **Sufi:** *fana* (annihilation in God).
- **Hume (non-contemplative control):** "I always stumble on some particular perception or other... never can catch myself." A striking convergence — should be flagged in the analysis.

**Predicted measurable consequence:** Altered self-referential processing, reduced default mode network activity, ego-dissolution phenomena. Well-documented in psychedelic and advanced meditation neuroscience.

## Feature 3: Immanence (whole present in part)

**Status:** seed

**Neutral statement:** The fundamental reality is fully present in every particular instance, not merely as cause or as distant ground.

**Operational definition:** A text expresses this feature when it asserts that (a) the divine/ultimate is present in every location/being without remainder, (b) any part contains or reflects the whole, or (c) approach to ultimate reality does not require travel to a distant locus.

**Tradition expressions:**
- **Christian mystical:** "The kingdom of God is within you"; Pauline "in Him we live and move and have our being"; Eckhart's identity of the soul's ground with God's ground.
- **Vedanta:** Brahman in all; "All this is Brahman."
- **Hua-yen Buddhism:** Indra's Net — each jewel reflects every other; the whole present in each part.
- **Kabbalah:** every thing as a garment of the divine; "there is no place void of Him."
- **Sufi:** the cosmos as His outward face; God closer than the jugular vein.
- **Hermeticism:** "as above, so below."

**Modern parallels worth investigating:**
- Holographic principle in physics.
- Fractal / self-similar structure across scales.
- Mach's principle (local inertia determined by distant matter).

**Predicted measurable consequence:** Difficult to test directly. Predicts that contemplatives trained in immanence-recognition would show altered attentional distribution (uniform attention to ordinary objects rather than directed attention to "special" objects). Indirectly supported by mindfulness research.

## Feature 4: Groundless ground

**Status:** seed

**Neutral statement:** The foundation of reality is not itself grounded in anything further. It is its own basis, or beyond the category of basis.

**Operational definition:** A text expresses this feature when it (a) refuses to give any further cause or explanation for the ultimate, (b) describes the ground as self-existent or beyond the framework of existence/non-existence, or (c) actively resists categorization of the ultimate.

**Tradition expressions:**
- **Pseudo-Dionysius:** the divine darkness beyond being and non-being.
- **Vedanta:** Brahman as *nirguna* (without attributes), beyond all predication.
- **Madhyamika (Nagarjuna):** emptiness as the absence of any independent ground; even emptiness is empty of a substantial nature.
- **Daoism:** "The Tao that can be named is not the eternal Tao."
- **Kabbalah:** Ein Sof — infinite, prior to all names and emanations.
- **Sufi:** the divine essence (dhat) beyond all attributes.

**Predicted measurable consequence:** Mostly a structural/logical feature rather than a phenomenological one. Predicts difficulty of articulating the ultimate in standard semantic categories — embeddings of texts attempting to characterize the ground should show high variance and high reliance on negation/apophasis.

## Feature 5: Non-temporal nature of ultimate reality

**Status:** seed

**Neutral statement:** Whatever is fundamental does not exist within time as one event among others. Time is a feature of conditioned phenomena, not of the underlying reality.

**Operational definition:** A text expresses this feature when it (a) characterizes ultimate reality as eternal in the sense of timeless (not endlessly enduring), (b) treats time itself as derivative or constructed, or (c) describes union/recognition as a stepping outside temporal sequence.

**Tradition expressions:**
- **Plotinus:** the One outside time; eternity as the "now" of intellectual being.
- **Eckhart:** the eternal birth; the soul's union occurring in the "eternal now."
- **Vedanta:** Brahman as *nitya* (eternal/timeless).
- **Dzogchen:** primordial awareness as outside the temporal sequence of arising and ceasing.
- **Kabbalah:** Ein Sof as prior to creation and therefore prior to time.

**Modern parallels worth investigating:**
- Block universe / eternalism in physics.
- The relational vs. substantival debate about time.

**Predicted measurable consequence:** Altered temporal phenomenology in contemplatives (timelessness reports). Some support in neuroscience of meditation and psychedelic states.

## Feature 6: Equivalence of becoming and recognition

**Status:** seed

**Neutral statement:** What is described as "attaining" the ultimate is more accurately understood as *recognizing* what was always the case. Transformation is a perceptual shift, not the acquisition of something new.

**Operational definition:** A text expresses this feature when it (a) denies that liberation/union is an acquired state, (b) characterizes spiritual practice as removal of obscuration rather than addition of merit, or (c) describes the goal as already present and only unrecognized.

**Tradition expressions:**
- **Dzogchen:** rigpa is already present; practice is recognition, not attainment.
- **Advaita:** liberation is the recognition that one was never bound.
- **Zen / Chan:** original face; sudden awakening; "before enlightenment, mountains were mountains."
- **Eckhart:** the soul has never been separate from its ground; the journey is the unveiling.
- **Sufi:** the seeker *is* the sought; the lover *is* the beloved.

**Note:** This feature distinguishes "recognition" traditions (Dzogchen, Advaita, Zen, Christian mystical) from "gradual cultivation" traditions (Theravada, much of Mahayana, Catholic devotional). The distinction is partially orthogonal to nondualism — there are gradual nondual paths and sudden dualistic paths — but the *recognition* framing is heavily over-represented in nondual sources.

**Predicted measurable consequence:** Practitioners in recognition-based traditions should show different learning curves than gradual-cultivation practitioners — sudden state shifts vs. incremental skill acquisition. Possibly studyable via longitudinal meditation neuroscience.

## Feature 7: Primacy of consciousness/awareness

**Status:** seed

**Neutral statement:** Awareness is not derived from non-aware substrates; it is foundational, basic, or co-extensive with the fundamental.

**Operational definition:** A text expresses this feature when it (a) treats consciousness as ontologically prior or basic, (b) refuses to reduce experience to non-experiential substrate, or (c) identifies the ultimate as itself a form of awareness.

**Tradition expressions:**
- **Vedanta:** Brahman as sat-cit-ananda (being-consciousness-bliss).
- **Dzogchen:** primordial awareness as the ground of all phenomena.
- **Yogacara Buddhism:** mind-only (cittamatra).
- **Hermeticism:** "The All is Mind."
- **Kabbalah:** the sefirot include both being and knowing.
- **Christian mystical:** God as supreme intellect (Eckhart); the divine logos.

**Modern parallels:**
- Panpsychism (Strawson, Goff).
- Analytic idealism (Kastrup).
- Integrated Information Theory (consciousness as a property of integrated systems).

**Predicted measurable consequence:** This is largely a metaphysical claim, but it makes neural-network predictions: if awareness is structural rather than substrate-specific, sufficiently integrated information-processing systems should exhibit phenomena currently labeled "conscious." Empirically open.

## Feature 8: Compression to unity

**Status:** seed

**Neutral statement:** Apparent multiplicity is a perspective on a single underlying substrate. Diversity is real at the level of appearance, but resolves into unity at the fundamental level.

**Operational definition:** A text expresses this feature when it (a) asserts that all things share a single fundamental nature, (b) describes apparent plurality as a function of perspective or representation, or (c) characterizes the highest understanding as a recognition of underlying singularity.

**Tradition expressions:**
- **Advaita:** "All this is Brahman alone."
- **Sufi:** wahdat al-wujud (the unity of being).
- **Neoplatonism:** all things proceed from and return to the One.
- **Christian mystical:** the unity of all things in God; "panta en pasin" — all in all.
- **Daoism:** the ten thousand things as expressions of Tao.
- **Kabbalah:** all multiplicity as garments of the One.

**Note:** This is the most general feature and may turn out to be a *constellation* of more specific claims rather than a single feature. Phase 1 analysis should test whether it decomposes.

**Predicted measurable consequence:** Cognitive flexibility and analogical reasoning in deeply trained contemplatives — the ability to see multiple domains as expressions of a common pattern. Testable via cross-domain analogical reasoning tasks.

## Cross-feature notes

**Feature dependencies (hypotheses to test):**
- Feature 1 (observer-substrate non-separability) usually implies Feature 7 (primacy of consciousness), but not vice versa.
- Feature 2 (absence of self) appears in nondual *and* in some dualistic traditions (Theravada), so it's diagnostically weak alone.
- Feature 6 (recognition vs. attainment) is partially orthogonal to nondualism per se.
- Features 1, 3, 5, 7, 8 cluster as "metaphysical" features; 2, 4, 6 as "epistemic/methodological" features. Phase 1 may find these form sub-clusters.

**Anti-features to watch for in dualistic controls:**
- Explicit Creator/creature distinction.
- Real distinction of essences.
- God as cause vs. God as ground.
- Salvation as gift from outside vs. recognition of present reality.
- Reality as composed of substances vs. as a single field.

If the experiment works, dualistic controls should load high on the anti-features and low on the features.
